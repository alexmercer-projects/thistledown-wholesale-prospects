01 · The brief

Who counts as a prospect, decided before the search.

Rules first, then research. Writing them down is what keeps a list from filling up with chains, closed shops and places too far away to deliver to.

In the list only if it is

  1. Independent. Franchise buying is decided by head office.
  2. Open now. Checked against a recent post, sign or listing.
  3. Inside the loop. 25 driving miles or less from the roastery.
  4. Serving or able to sell coffee. Including waiting rooms and front desks.
  5. Free to pick a supplier. No building-wide or public bid contracts.
  6. Not already a customer. Checked against the roaster's account list.

Every row has

  • Business, type, address
  • Contact name and role
  • Email (only if published) and phone
  • What coffee they use now
  • Fit score out of 10 and a tier
  • Why it fits, in one line
  • A first line for outreach
  • Source link and checked date
  • Status dropdown for follow-up

04 · Quality checks

What I checked so you don't have to.

A lead list is only worth something if the first call doesn't land on a closed shop or a chain. These are the checks every row went through, {RANGE}.

  • {N}/{N}rows link to the page the details came from, with the date it was checked.
  • {EMAILS}emails, each one published by the business. {FORMS} rows say “Contact form only” instead of a guessed address.
  • {DUPN}duplicates merged: same phone or address under an old name, an abbreviation or a second listing.
  • {SKIPN}places found and left off, each with the rule it failed, so nobody wastes time re-finding them.
